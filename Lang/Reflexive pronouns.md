
>A **reflexive pronoun** is a [pronoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pronoun "Pronoun") that refers to another noun or pronoun (its [antecedent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antecedent_\(grammar\) "Antecedent (grammar)")) within the same sentence.
>
>In the [English language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language") specifically, a reflexive pronoun will end in _-self_ or _-selves_, and refer to a previously named noun or pronoun (_myself_, _yourself_, _ourselves_, _themselves_, etc.). English [intensive pronouns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intensive_pronouns "Intensive pronouns"), used for emphasis, take the same form. [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflexive_pronoun | Reflexive Pronouns - Wikipedia]]

Like their[[Personal Pronouns#1st person | personal pronoun counterpart]], in the first person there is an inclusive/exclusive of the listener. (Us and you vs Us but not you)
