
Generally, only the nominative is used for questions, with other forms being used for relative clauses or adverbial phrases.


|      | Who   | What   | Where | When   | Which   | Why  | How   |
| ---- | ----- | ------ | ----- | ------ | ------- | ---- | ----- |
| Nom. | woka  | wez    | wau   | wef    | woeg    | wit  | huf   |
| Gen. | wokan | wezna  | waun  | wefen  | woegna  | witn | hufn  |
| Acc. | wokth | wezath | wauth | wefeth | woegtha | with | hufth |
| Dat. | wokas | wezas  | waus  | wefas  | woegas  | wits | hufs  |


### Nae

nae, when at the end of a sentence, turns a statement into a yes/no question. something akin to: "you like pickles, right?" You are stating something with the expectation the listener will confirm or contest it.
