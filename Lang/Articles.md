
All nouns in Wislandic are indefinite by default. as if there is an inherent "a" in front of them.

The definite article *Kon* works similarly to "the" in English, turning a noun definite.

The vocative article *i/ib* marks a noun as vocative, as you are directly addressing something or someone. It is not necessary when using a personal pronoun, but is often used in writing or formal contexts, or in poetry. It carries a meaning of invoking the thing you are calling out to. In some contexts, it is rude to not use it when talking to someone.

There is also the collective article, *el*, which marks the noun as a collective or conceptual or even a hypothetical.

In poetic contexts, *ib* and *el* will sometimes combine into *ibel* when one is talking to or invoking a concept or idea.

> [! Ex 1: Tanki]
> *Tanki* - A Child
> Kon Tanki - The Child
> El Tanki - Childness as a concept, or children broadly in the plural
> I Tanki - O' Child
> Ibel Tanki - O' Childhood
